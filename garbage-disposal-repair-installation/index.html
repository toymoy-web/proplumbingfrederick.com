Overview

How Garbage Disposal Repair and Installation Works in Frederick

Garbage disposal repair in Frederick begins with safety. The unit is switched off and unplugged, or its breaker is turned off, before anyone reaches near the blades. Never put a hand into a disposal, even when it is off. A hex key in the bottom socket turns the shaft safely and frees most jams.

A disposal usually lasts eight to twelve years. If the unit is leaking from its body, the motor is humming without turning after a jam is cleared, or it keeps tripping the breaker, replacing it makes more sense than repair. A new disposal comes in a few horsepower ratings, and a one-half or three-quarter horsepower unit suits most households.

Installation covers more than hanging the unit, and the International Plumbing Code (IPC) that Maryland follows governs the trap, the vent and the dishwasher connection. The mounting, the drain connection, the dishwasher hookup and the electrical supply all need to be right. A dishwasher drain connects through a knockout plug in the disposal that must be removed, or the dishwasher will not drain. Skipping that step is the most common installation error.

Use It Well

What Not to Do With a Disposal in Frederick: Items to Keep Out

  1. Grease, oil and fat. They cool in the pipe and cause clogs, and Frederick County's utility urges residents to keep them out of drains.
  2. Coffee grounds and eggshells in quantity, which pack into sludge in the trap.
  3. Bones, fruit pits and corn husks, which jam the blades or stringy fibers that wrap around them.
  4. Pasta, rice and potato peels, which swell and gum up the drain.
  5. Anything that is not food, including utensils, bottle caps and rubber bands.

Run cold water while the disposal works and for several seconds after, so the waste moves through the trap.
