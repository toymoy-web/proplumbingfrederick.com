Under the Sink

Five Things a Wet Sink Cabinet Can Mean

Drip from the faucet spout

A worn cartridge, washer or seat lets water past the closed valve.

Moisture on the supply lines

A loose nut or a cracked braided line leaks at the connection.

Water under the drain

A loose slip nut, cracked trap or failed gasket drips when the sink empties.

Spray from the handle

A worn o-ring or packing leaks around the stem.

Soft, warped cabinet floor

A slow leak has been wetting the wood for months.

Overview

How Faucet and Sink Repair Works in Frederick

Faucet and sink repair in Frederick is mostly about finding which part has failed. A kitchen faucet has a cartridge or valve, a spout seal and two supply lines. A sink drain has a strainer, a tailpiece, a trap and a wall connection. Each wears at a different rate, and each can drip in a way that looks like the next.

The technician tests under the sink with a dry paper towel and a flashlight. Wiping every joint dry and then running water shows exactly where the drip starts. Faucets from major brands take standard cartridges, and a replacement costs far less than a new fixture. If the faucet is old, corroded or no longer makes parts, replacement is better value.

Since 2014 federal rules limit lead in plumbing that carries drinking water, so a new faucet is also a chance to move away from older lead-bearing brass. Look for products certified to NSF/ANSI 61, the standard for drinking water contact.

Frederick Water

What Roughly 100 mg/L Does to a Faucet

Frederick city water averages about 100 mg/L of hardness, which is slightly hard. Over the years, that leaves white scale on aerators and showerheads and slows flow to a trickle. A fifteen-minute soak in vinegar often restores an aerator, and it is the first step before replacing a faucet that seems weak.

Homes on private wells can have much harder water and iron, which stains sinks orange and clogs cartridges faster. If you see staining or frequent scale, ask about a water softener before replacing fixtures over and over.

Hidden Cabinet Leaks

A slow under-sink leak that has soaked a cabinet for months is often excluded as long-term seepage. A sudden break from a failed supply line is different. Photograph the cabinet and the failed part before cleanup.
