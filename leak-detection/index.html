My Water Bill Jumped and Nothing Is Dripping. Where Is the Water Going?

A bill that doubles with no visible leak is one of the most common reasons people search for help. Water may be escaping from a pipe in a wall, under a floor, in the yard between the meter and the house, or into a toilet that never stops running. Finding the source without opening everything is a job called leak detection.

Emergency

Emergency Leak Detection: Active Leaks That Cannot Wait

A water meter that keeps moving with everything off

If the meter dial turns after every fixture is closed, water is leaving somewhere. Shut the main valve and call if the reading keeps running.

Water coming up in the yard or the driveway

A wet patch, a soft spot or a stretch of unusually green grass over the service line usually marks a buried leak.

A stain spreading across a ceiling or wall

A wet ceiling can mean a supply line, a drain or a fixture above. Shut off the main first, then call so the source is found before drywall comes down.

A utility alert or a bill that suddenly multiplied

A leak notice from the water utility or a bill that doubles is a signal to test the meter today, not next month.

First Steps

Emergency Leak Detection: What to Do Before We Arrive

  1. Shut off the main and read the meter

    Close the main valve, then check the meter dial. If it still moves, the leak is between the meter and the house. If it stops, the leak is inside.

  2. Note when and where you see signs

    Write down the stain location, the sound and when it started. Detection is faster when the crew knows where to begin.

  3. Leave the walls alone

    Do not cut drywall to look. Acoustic, thermal and camera tools usually find the pipe without opening a wall.

Try This First

A Meter Test You Can Do in Ten Minutes

  1. Stop all water use

    Turn off every faucet, the dishwasher, the washer and the ice maker. Make sure nobody flushes.

  2. Read the meter

    Write down the number, or note the position of the small dial or triangle on the meter face.

  3. Wait and read again

    Wait 15 to 30 minutes with no water use, then read it again.

  4. Compare

    If the reading moved, water is leaking somewhere on the property. If not, the problem may be intermittent.

Overview

How Leak Detection Works in Frederick

Leak detection in Frederick starts by working out which part of the plumbing is losing water. A meter test separates leaks inside the house from leaks in the service line between the meter and the foundation. A pressure test of the supply system narrows it further. Only then does the technician use listening gear or a thermal camera.

The goal is to find a single pipe, not a general area. A leak found within a foot or two means one small opening in a wall or one trench in a yard, instead of tearing out a whole room. That precision is what you pay for, and it is cheaper than guesswork.

After a repair, the supply piping is pressure tested, which is what the International Plumbing Code (IPC) expects for new or altered pipe. If the leak is in a supply line that has been failing for years, the answer may be a small repair or a longer-term fix such as repiping. If the leak is outside, water line repair and replacement covers the pipe that runs from the meter.

Frederick Specifics

The City's Leak Forgiveness Program and What It Requires

The City of Frederick offers a one-time reduction on water and sewer charges when a leak causes an excessive bill. The city looks for a bill at least twice the normal usage, based on the average of the last three billing cycles, and the program is for residential customers. It is not offered to apartments or condominiums served by one meter.

The city expects the leak to be repaired first. After the repair, the Water Department takes two readings to confirm usage has returned to normal, and the adjustment runs from the first day of the protested bill to the first normal reading. A written leak report from a technician gives you a clear record of what failed and when it was fixed.

That makes timing matter. A homeowner who waits months before investigating a high bill loses bargaining room and pays for more water. If your bill has jumped and your address is in 21701, 21702, 21703 or 21704, run the meter test above and call.

Safety

What to Do Before Anyone Opens a Wall

Hidden leaks sit near wiring, gas lines and structure. Keep these in mind while you wait.

  1. Do not cut into drywall or flooring on a guess. Wiring and gas piping often run next to water lines.
  2. If water is near the electrical panel, outlets or a light fixture, switch off power to that area from a dry spot or call before touching anything.
  3. Shut off the main if you can hear water running and cannot find the source.
  4. Do not drill or cut a concrete slab to look for a leak. Locate it first.
  5. Photograph stains and take daily meter readings so the technician sees how fast water is escaping.

Then call and share the readings. They save time on site.

Warning Signs

Signs of a Leak You Cannot See

Meter moves with everything off

This is the surest sign of a leak somewhere on the property.

Sound of running water at night

A hiss or trickle when the house is quiet can mean a pressurized line is leaking.

Warm spot on the floor

A hot water line under a slab or floor can warm the surface above it.

Damp or musty smell

Moisture trapped in a wall or crawlspace often smells before it shows.

Stains or peeling paint

Water moving through drywall leaves brown rings and bubbles the finish.

Lower water pressure than usual

A leak can drop pressure, especially in older galvanized supply lines.

Slab and Basement Homes

Slab Leaks, Crawlspace Leaks and Where Frederick Houses Differ

Most older Frederick houses sit on basements or crawlspaces, so supply lines are visible overhead and leaks are easier to reach than in a slab-on-grade home. Slab leaks do occur in newer townhomes, additions and some subdivisions, where supply lines were run in or under the concrete. For those, tracer gas and acoustic tools find the spot before any concrete is cut.

Commercial properties have the same problem on a larger scale. A shop, restaurant or office with a rising water bill needs the same meter test, usually followed by zone-by-zone isolation. See commercial plumbing service for larger systems.

What Insurance Looks For

Many policies cover sudden, accidental leaks and the damage they cause, but not the cost of finding or repairing a slow leak. Some policies cover tear-out to reach a leak. Ask your agent before work starts, and keep the detection report with the moisture readings.

Local Notes

Leak Detection in Frederick: Slabs, Masonry and Basements

A hidden leak in a downtown brick building can travel along masonry before it shows on a ceiling, so the wet spot is often far from the pipe. In newer Frederick subdivisions, slab and crawlspace lines are the usual suspects.

Homes in low spots near Carroll Creek and Baker Park can show damp walls that are not plumbing at all. Detection tells you which problem you have before you pay for the wrong repair.

Where Leaks Hide

Where Frederick Callers Find Leaks: Ceilings, Slabs, Yards and Irrigation

Ceilings and walls

A stain under a bathroom, a bubbling paint patch or a damp wall can come from a supply line, a drain or a shower valve above. Detection traces it before anyone opens drywall.

Under a slab or in a crawlspace

Warm spots on a floor, a meter that runs with the house shut down or the sound of water under a slab point to a leak in or under the foundation.

The line from the meter to the house

Wet ground, a patch of lush grass or a pressure drop often marks a buried service line. Long runs from the street or a well are common in the county.

Irrigation, pools and hot tubs

A sprinkler zone that runs weakly, a valve box full of water or a pool that loses water without evaporation can be tested separately from the house.

Apartments, triplexes and commercial buildings

A multi-unit property needs the leak isolated unit by unit, and a parking lot with water coming up needs line tracing. Tell us the building type when you call.

A home that is for sale or about to be

Buyers and agents often ask for a leak check before closing. Detection gives a documented answer on whether water is escaping.
