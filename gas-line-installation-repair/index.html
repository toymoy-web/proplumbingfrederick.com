Safety First

If You Smell Gas, Do This Before Anything Else

Natural gas is treated with an odor so leaks are noticed. Never wait to see if the smell passes.

  1. Leave the house at once with everyone inside, including pets. Do not stop to open windows.
  2. Do not switch lights or appliances on or off, and do not use a phone inside the house.
  3. From outside or a neighbor's house, call 911 and then your gas utility's emergency number.
  4. Stay out until the utility or fire department says it is safe to return.
  5. Call this line afterward for the repair. A plumber cannot work on a line until the utility has made the area safe.

A faint smell near a stove can be a pilot problem, but treat every gas odor as urgent.

Overview

How Gas Line Installation and Repair Works in Frederick

Gas line installation and repair in Frederick covers the piping that runs from the meter or the tank to each appliance. Washington Gas serves many Frederick County households, and its meter marks the point where the utility's pipe ends and the house piping begins. Everything past the meter, including new lines for a range, dryer, fireplace or outdoor grill, is work for a licensed plumber and gas fitter.

Maryland's master plumber license is a combined plumbing and gas fitter license, and it allows the holder to install, repair and replace gas piping and the connections to gas appliances. The state plumbing code adopts the International Fuel Gas Code (IFGC), which covers pipe sizing, materials, shutoff valves and testing. That is why sizing matters: a pipe that is too small starves an appliance at peak demand.

A typical job starts with a count of appliances and their BTU ratings. The technician sizes the pipe, runs it, installs a shutoff valve at each appliance, and pressure tests the whole line before the utility or the plumber turns the gas on.

Repair or Replace

When an Old Gas Line Is Repaired and When It Is Replaced

A repair is reasonable when

  • One fitting or valve is leaking.
  • The pipe is otherwise sound and properly sized.
  • A flex connector at an appliance has aged.
  • A pressure test finds a single weak joint.

Replacement is the better choice when

  • Pipe is badly corroded or has multiple weak joints.
  • A new appliance needs a larger line than the old one.
  • Old connectors are corrugated brass of unknown age.
  • The system fails a pressure test in several places.

A shutoff valve at each appliance is standard practice and makes repairs safer.
