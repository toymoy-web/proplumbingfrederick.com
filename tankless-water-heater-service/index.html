Tankless Heater Showing an Error Code, Going Lukewarm or Cutting Out?

A tankless unit gives you very little warning. One week the water is hot, and the next it fades to lukewarm halfway through a shower, or a code flashes and the burner shuts off. In most cases the heater is not worn out. It is choked with mineral scale or starved of gas or water, and the cure is tankless water heater service.

Overview

How Tankless Water Heater Service Works in Frederick

Tankless water heater service in Frederick is mostly maintenance, and the main job is removing scale. Cold water enters the unit, passes over a small, hot heat exchanger and leaves heated. Minerals in the water bake onto that heat exchanger and narrow the channels. The heater has to work harder, the flow drops, and eventually a sensor shuts it off.

Gas models add three more checks: the gas supply, the burner and the vent, which the International Fuel Gas Code (IFGC) covers in Maryland. A unit that was installed on a gas line that is too small will fire weakly under demand, even when it is clean. A blocked or cracked vent can trigger a safety shutoff, and a condensing model has a drain and a neutralizer cartridge that clog or run out.

Many owners learn that service is needed only when the heater fails. The better path is a yearly flush, and water heater replacement information is useful if the unit is past its expected life.

Service Visit

What a Flush and Inspection Includes

  1. Check the history

    The technician asks about error codes, water temperature and how long since the last flush.

  2. Isolate and flush

    Service valves are closed, a pump circulates descaling solution through the heat exchanger and the unit is rinsed.

  3. Clean the filters

    The cold water inlet screen and any other filter are cleaned or replaced.

  4. Test and inspect

    Gas pressure, burner, venting, condensate drain and safety devices are tested, and the unit is run through its cycle.

Who Does What

Owner Tasks and Technician Tasks

An owner can usually

  • Clean the cold water inlet screen.
  • Check that the vent is clear outside.
  • Note the error code before calling.
  • Keep the area around the unit clear.

A technician should handle

  • Descaling with a circulation pump.
  • Gas pressure and burner checks.
  • Heat exchanger and flow sensor repairs.
  • Venting corrections and condensate service.

Gas work and combustion checks belong to a licensed professional. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) also expects a relief valve and a discharge pipe on the unit, and your owner's manual lists the service schedule the maker expects.

Frederick Water

Why Roughly 100 mg/L Still Calls for a Yearly Flush

City of Frederick water averages about 100 mg/L of hardness, or roughly six grains per gallon, which is slightly hard. A tankless heater has no big tank to settle that scale, so it coats the heat exchanger directly. Most makers call for a flush every year, and some ask for more in harder water. Skipping it can also affect the warranty.

Homes outside the city on private wells often have harder water, plus iron or sediment, and scale builds faster. In those homes a water softener ahead of the tankless unit protects it far better than frequent flushing.

Gas supply matters too. Washington Gas serves many Frederick County homes, and tankless units draw far more gas at peak than a tank. A house that was sized for a tank heater may need a larger gas line before a tankless unit performs as advertised. A technician checks line size and pressure as part of the visit.

Safety

When to Call: A Tankless Problem That Is Not Just a Nuisance

Gas appliances can fail in ways that matter. Treat these as urgent.

  1. The smell of gas sends everyone out of the house. Make the 911 call and the call to the gas utility from the yard, not from inside.
  2. A carbon monoxide alarm sounding near the heater means leaving the house and calling 911.
  3. Water around the unit or on the floor beneath it should be stopped. Close the supply valves and the gas valve if you can reach them safely.
  4. Soot, a yellow flame or a vent pipe that is loose, disconnected or corroded needs prompt service.
  5. Repeated shutdowns with a venting code should not be reset again and again. Stop and call.

For a non-urgent problem, note the code and the date and call for a daytime visit.
