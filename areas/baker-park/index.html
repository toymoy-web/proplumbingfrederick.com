The Area

Plumber for the Baker Park Area of Frederick

The City of Frederick acquired the first land for the park in 1916, built the facilities in 1926 and 1927 and dedicated the park in June 1927. It honors Joseph Dill Baker, who offered funds toward the land. The park runs from Bentz Street west to West College Terrace.

Real estate guides describe the surrounding neighborhood as historic single-family homes, rowhouses and updated properties, many dating to the early twentieth century. We found no official boundary map, so this page uses the park and nearby streets such as Rosemont Avenue as anchors, with ZIP 21701.

Older Homes

Honest Expectations for Older and Renovated Homes

A home's age alone does not tell you what is inside the walls. Houses near the park have been renovated over the years, so one may have copper, another plastic and another a mix of materials.

The reliable way to know is to look. A plumber can check the main shutoff, the supply lines in the basement and the water heater, and can run a camera down the drain to see the sewer line from the house.

Mature trees are part of what makes the area attractive. If you have slow drains or gurgling, a camera inspection is the dependable way to learn whether the cause is inside the house or on the line outside.

Buyers and Renovators

Before You Buy or Renovate a Home Near the Park

  1. Find the main shutoff

    Locate it, turn it off and on, and note if it is stiff or leaks at the stem.

  2. Check water pressure

    A simple gauge on a hose bib shows whether pressure is steady.

  3. Read the water heater

    The data plate shows its age. Plan ahead if it is near the end of its life.

  4. Camera the sewer line

    Ask for a recorded inspection before closing or before a remodel.

  5. Ask about past permits

    Ask the seller what plumbing work was done and whether it was permitted.
