Wet Spot in the Yard, Low Pressure or a Letter About Lead Pipes? Check the Water Service Line

A soggy patch of lawn with no rain, a drop in pressure at every tap or a letter about your service line material all point to the buried pipe that brings water to the house. Repairing or replacing it is called water line repair and replacement, and it is separate from the plumbing inside the walls.

Overview

How Water Line Repair and Replacement Works in Frederick

A water service line has two sections. The utility owns the pipe from the main to the meter. The customer owns the pipe from the meter to the structure. That second section is the one this work covers, and it is buried below the frost line, which Frederick County's code sets at 30 inches.

A repair fixes a leak with a coupling and a short piece of new pipe, which works on a sound line with a single failure. A full replacement runs new pipe, usually copper or PEX rated for burial, from the meter to the house. Trenchless methods can pull a new pipe through the old path with less digging, where conditions allow.

Under the county's permit rules, a licensed master plumber is the only person who may install the line between the main and the house, and the job needs a permit and an inspection. Call 811 before digging so buried utilities are marked.

The City's Lead Survey

What the City of Frederick Is Asking Homeowners to Check

Under federal Lead and Copper Rule requirements, the City of Frederick has released an inventory of service line materials by address, listed as non-lead, lead, galvanized requiring replacement or unknown. The city asked homeowners, especially those with homes built before 1973, to check their lines and report what they find.

A service line that is lead, or galvanized and downstream of lead, is slated for replacement. An unknown line should be treated as lead until verified. A scratch test and a magnet help identify the pipe at the meter: lead scratches easily and is not magnetic, while galvanized steel is magnetic. A plumber can confirm, and a certified filter reduces exposure until the line is replaced.

Who Pays

Standard homeowners insurance usually does not pay to replace a worn service line, though some utilities and insurers sell service line coverage. Check what is available before assuming the cost is yours alone.

Local Notes

Water Line Repair From the Street to Your Frederick Home

The line from the curb to the house runs under lawns, brick sidewalks and driveways in the Historic District and under newer lots along the Golden Mile. A leak shows as a soggy lawn, a hissing meter or a sudden drop in pressure.

Frederick's city meter marks the end of the city's responsibility. Everything from the meter to the house is the owner's, so a break in an older block can be a significant repair.
