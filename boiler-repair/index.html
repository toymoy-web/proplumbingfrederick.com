Six Checks Before You Call About a Dead Boiler

These take a few minutes and sometimes solve the problem. Stop if anything smells like gas or smoke.

  1. Confirm the thermostat is set to heat and the batteries are fresh.
  2. Check that the boiler switch and the breaker are on. Many boilers have a service switch near the stairs.
  3. Look at the pressure gauge. A hot water boiler usually sits near 12 to 15 psi when cold. Near zero means it has lost water.
  4. Check the fuel. An oil tank may be empty, and a gas boiler needs the gas valve open and the pilot or igniter working.
  5. Look for water on the floor around the boiler or under baseboard and radiator valves.
  6. Press the reset button once only. If the burner locks out again, leave it off and call.

A gas odor or smoke means you leave the house right away and call 911 first. This line is the second call.

Emergency

Emergency Boiler Repair: No Heat and Leaks

No heat in cold weather

A boiler that will not fire, a dead circulator or a stuck zone valve is urgent in a Frederick winter. Pipes can freeze in a cold house.

Water leaking from the boiler or piping

A leak from the tank, the relief valve or a pipe joint should be checked now. Shut the water feed to the boiler.

A pressure gauge that is too high or too low

Pressure above the safe range opens the relief valve. Pressure that keeps dropping means water is leaving.

Banging, kettling or a smell of fuel

Strange noises can point to scale or low water. A fuel smell means leave the house and call the gas utility.

Overview

How Boiler Repair Works in Frederick

Boiler repair in Frederick covers the heating systems found in older homes across the city, from cast iron radiators in century-old rowhouses to baseboard hot water loops in mid-century ranches. A boiler heats water or makes steam and sends it through pipes to radiators or baseboard units. When it stops, the cause is usually a failed ignition, a lost water supply, a stuck circulator pump or a leak.

The technician starts with the same questions every time. Is it a hot water system or steam? What fuel does it burn? Does it run at all, and what do the pressure gauge and temperature gauge say? From there the diagnosis follows a known path, which is why a good description on the phone saves time on site.

Frederick homes burn natural gas from Washington Gas, oil from a tank or propane. Each fuel has its own failure points, so the first look is at the burner and the flame, then at the water side. If the problem is a leak in the heating piping rather than the boiler, emergency leak detection finds it before walls are opened.

What Your Boiler Is Saying

Noises and Symptoms That Point to a Specific Fault

Banging or hammering in steam pipes

Water is trapped in a pipe that is not pitched back to the boiler, or a radiator valve is stuck partly shut.

Kettle-like rumbling

Scale or sediment on the heat exchanger is boiling the water in pockets. It wears the boiler and wastes fuel.

Frequent on and off cycling

A boiler that is oversized, has a faulty thermostat or has low water will short cycle and wear out its parts.

Radiators warm at the bottom, cold on top

Air trapped in the system blocks hot water. Bleeding the radiators usually fixes it.

Pressure drops every few days

The system is losing water somewhere, or the expansion tank has failed.

Water dripping from the relief valve line

The system is over-pressure or the valve has failed. Leave the line open and have it looked at.

System Types

Hot Water Boilers and Steam Boilers Fail Differently

Hot water (hydronic) systems

  • Circulator pump seizes or loses its bearings.
  • Expansion tank loses its air charge.
  • Air traps in baseboards and radiators.
  • Pressure falls from leaks or a bad fill valve.
  • Zone valves stick closed.

Steam systems

  • Low water cutoff fouls and shuts the boiler off.
  • Radiator vents clog and stop heat reaching them.
  • Water hammer from poorly pitched pipes.
  • Gauge glass and sight tubes scale over.
  • Return lines corrode and leak.

Both need yearly service. A boiler that has not been cleaned in years is more likely to fail on the coldest night.

Know Who Is Licensed

Heating Work and Plumbing Work Are Licensed Separately in Maryland

Maryland licenses heating contractors through the Board of Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractors, and hydronic heating is one of the categories on that board's license. Plumbing work is licensed by the State Board of Plumbing. The two boards cover different parts of a boiler job.

The practical result is that a boiler job can involve both trades. The burner, controls and combustion side belong to the heating license. The water supply piping, relief valve discharge, backflow preventer and gas piping to the boiler are plumbing work. Before a technician touches your system, ask who holds the Maryland HVACR license for the heating work and who holds the plumbing license for the piping.

A leak in a hot water line, a failed fill valve or a corroded fitting is plumbing, and that is where this line starts. A replacement relief valve must carry the pressure rating stamped on the boiler, and its discharge piping follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) that Maryland adopts. If the diagnosis points to the burner or controls, the dispatcher will tell you plainly who handles that part.

Frozen Pipes After a Heating Failure

A boiler failure in a cold house can freeze pipes within a day or two. Many policies cover freeze damage only if you took reasonable steps to keep the house heated. Keep records of when the heat failed and when you called.

Local Notes

Boiler Repair for Frederick's Older Hot-Water Heating

Radiator and baseboard boilers still heat many older houses around the Historic District and the blocks near Mount Olivet Cemetery. The common failures are a dead circulator, a stuck relief valve and a pilot that will not hold.

Boilers in these homes sit in stone or brick basements where damp air shortens the life of controls. Have the unit looked at in early autumn rather than the first cold night.
