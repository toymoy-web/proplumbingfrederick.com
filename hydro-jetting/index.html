Does Your Drain Clog Again a Few Weeks After Every Snaking?

A line that clears and then slows down again is usually not clogged by one thing. Grease, scale or roots are coating the pipe wall, and a cable only opens a narrow channel through the middle. The fix for that kind of buildup is to wash the whole pipe, which is what hydro jetting does.

Overview

How Hydro Jetting Works in Frederick

Hydro jetting in Frederick uses a hose fitted with a nozzle that fires water backward and sideways as it is fed into the pipe. The jets pull the nozzle forward and scour the inside wall, pushing debris toward the cleanout or the main. A pump delivers the water at pressure measured in thousands of pounds per square inch, much higher than a garden hose.

The technician does not start with the jet. A camera goes in first to check the pipe, because high-pressure water belongs only in a pipe that can take it. Once the line is judged sound, the jetter makes passes of the full length. A second camera run afterward shows the clean pipe wall and any damage that the buildup was hiding.

Jetting is the right tool for heavy kitchen grease, scale in old cast iron and root-choked lines. It is the wrong tool for a collapsed or broken pipe, and in that case sewer line repair service is the correct next step.

Maryland follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which expects drain and sewer piping to stay accessible for cleaning, and that access is what lets the hose in without opening a wall.

Jetting Day

From Camera to Clean Pipe

  1. Inspect first

    A camera checks pipe material, joints and obvious cracks before any pressure is applied.

  2. Set up access

    The jet hose enters through a cleanout or an opened fixture, with a drain path planned for the debris.

  3. Jet the line

    The nozzle scours the pipe wall in passes, working from the far end back toward the house.

  4. Confirm and report

    A second camera run shows the result, and you hear whether the pipe needs further work.

Good and Bad Candidates

Which Pipes Take Jetting and Which Should Be Left Alone

Jetting works well on

  • Cast iron lined with scale.
  • Kitchen and restaurant lines packed with grease.
  • Root growth in sound clay or plastic.
  • Main drains that clog repeatedly.
  • Lines that have no cracks or collapsed sections.

Jetting is a risk on

  • Orangeburg fiber pipe.
  • Clay with broken or offset joints.
  • Cast iron that is thin and flaking.
  • Sections already collapsed.
  • Pipes where the camera cannot pass.

The camera decides. A good technician will tell you if jetting would make a bad pipe worse.

Frederick Specifics

Grease, Roots and Old Iron in Frederick Lines

Downtown Frederick packs restaurants and shops into buildings with old drains, and grease is the common culprit. A cable leaves a thin film of that grease on the pipe wall, and the next meal adds to it. Jetting scours the film away, which is why it is the usual answer for kitchen lines that clog every few months.

In the older neighborhoods of 21701 and 21702, cast iron drains are often a century old and lined with scale. Roots from mature street trees find the joints in clay laterals and form a mat that returns every season. Jetting cuts the mat, though a lasting fix may still need a joint repair or a liner.

Commercial kitchens and apartment buildings do best with scheduled jetting on a calendar, since a service every six to twelve months costs less than an emergency. Details for larger systems are on the emergency commercial plumbing page.

Is Jetting Covered?

Routine line cleaning is maintenance and is rarely covered. If a backup has already damaged the house, the cleanup falls under a water or sewer backup endorsement, if you have one. Ask your agent before work begins.

Preparation

Getting Ready for a Jetting Visit

  1. Clear a path to the cleanout, the basement floor drain or the fixture that will be used for access.
  2. Move stored items away from the work area, because debris can splash during a pass.
  3. Tell the technician about previous clogs, root problems and any past repairs to the line.
  4. Keep children and pets out of the work area and away from the jet hose.
  5. Avoid running water in the house during the pass unless the technician asks you to.

Afterward, ask for the before and after camera footage and keep it with your house records.
