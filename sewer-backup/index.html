First Ten Minutes

Sewage Is Coming Up: Do These Things Now

Backed-up wastewater carries bacteria. Protect people first, property second.

  1. Stop all water use. No flushing, showers, dishwasher or washing machine, because every gallon goes to the same blocked line.
  2. Keep children, pets and anyone with a weak immune system out of the affected room.
  3. Do not wade through standing sewage in bare feet. If you must enter, wear rubber boots and gloves.
  4. Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into a backed-up line. It will not clear a main blockage and it harms whoever works on the pipe next.
  5. If water stands near outlets or appliances, leave the power alone unless you can reach the panel while dry.
  6. Open windows for air, and photograph the water level and damage before anything is moved.

Then call the emergency line. Say which fixtures are backing up and whether the water is still rising.

Overview

How Emergency Sewer Backup Repair Works in Frederick

Emergency sewer backup repair in Frederick begins with one question: where is the blockage? If the lowest drain in the house is overflowing, the problem is usually in the main line or the lateral to the street. If only one bathroom is backing up, it may be a branch line. The dispatcher uses your answers to decide what the technician brings.

On arrival the technician cuts through the blockage, usually from a cleanout, and flushes the line until water drains freely. A camera then runs the pipe to find what caused the backup. A one-time cause, such as a clump of wipes, leaves the pipe sound. A root mass, a crushed joint or a belly in the line will bring the backup back.

Clearing the line is plumbing work. Pumping out standing water and disinfecting floors is a separate job handled by a restoration crew, and water damage restoration service explains how that works.

Insurance

Why a Sewer Backup Often Is Not Covered, and What to Check

The Maryland Insurance Administration tells homeowners that water backing up through sewers or drains, when it is not due to the insured's negligence, is typically not covered under a standard homeowners policy. The same agency says you can buy water and sewer backup coverage, which also protects against a sump pump overflowing. Flood and groundwater seepage are separate exclusions.

After Tropical Storm Isabel in 2003, the same agency reported that it had become apparent many homeowners did not understand what their policies covered. If you are unsure that your policy has backup coverage, read the declarations page today and ask your agent for the exact wording and the limit.

If you do have the endorsement, it usually pays for cleanup and damaged belongings up to a limit, but not for replacing the sewer pipe itself. Photograph everything, keep the technician's invoice and the camera findings, and report the loss promptly.

Staying Safe

What Not to Do While Waiting for the Technician

  1. Do not use a wet vacuum on sewage unless it is rated for it, because it can spread bacteria through the exhaust.
  2. Do not mix bleach with other cleaners, since some combinations release toxic gas.
  3. Do not touch food, pets' bowls or dishes that sewage has reached. Throw them out.
  4. Do not walk from the wet area through clean rooms with the same shoes.
  5. Do not wash soaked clothing with the family laundry until the washer drain is confirmed clear.

Wash your hands well after any contact, and tell your doctor if anyone feels ill afterward.

Prevention

Backwater Valves and Other Ways to Stop a Repeat

A backwater valve is a one-way gate in the building drain. When the public sewer surcharges, the flap closes and keeps sewage from rising into your lowest fixtures. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) addresses this risk by calling for backwater protection on fixtures set below the next upstream manhole cover in the public sewer. Basements are the usual candidates.

The valve has limits. It needs an access cover so it can be cleaned, and it must be checked once a year because debris can keep the flap from sealing. It also does not help with a clog inside your own pipes. It guards against flow coming back from the street.

Other measures cost far less. Keep grease and wipes out of the drain, have a camera run done on a line that has backed up twice, and keep a cleanout at the property line accessible. A lateral with roots needs a sewer line repair, not another snaking.

Early Warnings

Signs a Backup Is Coming Before It Arrives

Gurgling from drains

Air is being pushed through water in a partial blockage.

Toilet bubbles when you run the washer

Wastewater from the washer has nowhere to go and pushes air back through the toilet.

Water rising in the lowest drain

The basement floor drain is the first outlet when the main is blocked.

Sewer smell indoors

A dry trap or a leaking pipe can release gas into the house.

Slow draining everywhere

When every fixture is slow, the problem is downstream of all of them.

Wet spot on the lawn

A cracked lateral can leak wastewater before it backs up.
