Faucets Sputtering, No Water at All, or the Pump Clicking Constantly? It May Be the Well Pump

When the water stops on a private well, there is no city main to blame. The pump in the well, the pressure tank in the basement and the switch between them are yours. A pump that cannot keep up, a tank that has lost its air charge or a failed switch all show up as the same symptoms, which is why well pump repair starts with a few tests.

Overview

How Well Pump Repair Works in Frederick

Well pump repair in Frederick covers the equipment between the well and your fixtures. A submersible pump sits far down in the well casing and pushes water up a drop pipe. A pressure tank in the house holds a cushion of pressurized water, and a pressure switch turns the pump on and off. A check valve holds water in the system when the pump is off.

The technician starts above ground, because most failures are there. The breaker and wiring are tested, the gauge and switch are checked and the tank air charge is measured. If those test sound, the pump motor is tested electrically. Pulling a pump from a deep well takes equipment and two people, so the diagnosis comes first.

The plumbing connections to the pressure tank follow the International Plumbing Code (IPC) that Maryland adopts. A well pump typically lasts ten to fifteen years. Frederick County has a large share of homes on private wells, especially in rural areas outside the towns. The health department's Well and Septic Branch keeps records on wells, so ask if you need to know the depth or the age.

Know the License

Who May Repair a Well Pump in Maryland

Maryland licenses well work through the State Board of Well Drillers at the Department of the Environment. Constructing or deconstructing a well takes a licensed well driller. State guidance says well pump replacement or modification can be done by a licensed well driller, a pump installer or a master plumber.

That is why a plumber handles pump and pressure tank work on many Frederick County wells, while drilling, deepening or sealing a well is a driller's job. Ask for the license number of whoever replaces the pump. The geology also varies, with Blue Ridge, Piedmont and carbonate regions across the county, so well depth and water quality change from one property to the next.
