Slow Drains, Gurgling Pipes and a Soggy Yard? A Septic Tank May Need Pumping

In a home on a septic system, every drain empties into a buried tank. When the tank fills with solids, drains slow, pipes gurgle and wastewater can surface in the yard. The fix for a full tank is pumping it out, and this page explains how that works and where the plumbing begins.

Overview

How Septic Tank Pumping Works in Frederick

Septic tank pumping in Frederick is done by a licensed liquid waste hauler. The Frederick County Health Department's Well and Septic Branch licenses haulers and handles permits for septic repairs. A plumber does not pump tanks, and a hauler does not repair house plumbing, so a good outcome often needs both.

Most guidance puts pumping at every three to five years, depending on tank size and household use. A family of five with a garbage disposal fills a tank faster than a couple. The tank must be located, its lid opened, the solids pumped out and the baffles inspected. Baffles that are cracked or missing let solids reach the drain field, which can ruin it.

The plumber's part is everything that connects to the tank: the main drain out of the house, the cleanout, the line to the tank and any fixtures that backed up. A camera run shows whether the line from the house is blocked or broken, which no amount of pumping will fix.

Protect the System

Habits That Keep a Septic System Healthy

  1. Spread out laundry across the week instead of running many loads in one day.
  2. Keep grease, wipes, hygiene products and chemicals out of the drains.
  3. Do not drive or park over the tank or the drain field.
  4. Keep records of every pumping and inspection with the house file.
  5. Fix leaking toilets and faucets promptly, because constant extra water overloads the field.

A full tank gives warnings. Know what to do when drains slow: cut water use and call early. The State Plumbing Code, built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), governs the pipes into the tank, and the county health department governs the tank and the field.
