Boiler Leaking in Winter: What Frederick Homeowners Should Check

By the Frederick Pro Plumbing team. Published 2026-10-03. Updated 2026-10-03.

Shut off the boiler, find where the water comes from and read the pressure gauge. A drip from the relief valve line means over-pressure, and a leak from the body means trouble.

The Short Answer

When a boiler leaks in winter, switch it off at the service switch, then look for the source. Water at a valve stem or a pipe joint is often a small repair. Water dripping from the relief valve discharge pipe means pressure is too high or the valve has failed. Water running from the boiler body, especially at the bottom, usually means the heat exchanger has cracked and the boiler may need replacing.

Do not cap a relief valve line. It is a safety device. Read the pressure gauge: a hot water boiler normally sits around 12 to 15 psi when cold and rises when hot. A gauge near zero means the system has lost water, and a gauge near the valve's rating, often 30 psi, means it is over-pressure.

Local Note

Boilers in Frederick Winters

Hot water boilers are common in older Frederick houses, and a leak often appears during the first hard cold. Homes in Middletown and Thurmont may also be on oil or propane, so check the fuel supply as well as the leak.

Shut off the boiler and feed water if you can do so safely, then see emergency boiler repair.

Who Is Licensed

Which Professional Handles a Leaking Boiler in Maryland

Maryland licenses heating work, hydronic heating included, through the Board of Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractors. The State Board of Plumbing licenses plumbing, and a boiler job often involves both: the burner and controls on the heating side, the water and relief piping on the plumbing side.

That affects who you call. A leaking pipe joint, a failed fill valve or a relief valve discharge line is plumbing work. A burner that will not fire or a control board that has failed is heating work. Ask the contractor which license covers each part.

In the mountain-side foothills near Thurmont, boilers run harder and longer through winter, so leaks tend to show up as the cold deepens. Do not wait for a drip to become a flood.

Triage

What to Do in the First Fifteen Minutes

  1. Switch it off

    Use the service switch or breaker, and close the fuel valve if you can.

  2. Find the leak

    Wipe joints dry and watch where water returns.

  3. Read the gauge

    Note the pressure now and again in ten minutes.

  4. Contain the water

    Put a pan or towels under the drip and lay a tarp if it is large.

  5. Protect the house

    If the house will be cold, open cabinet doors and keep faucets dripping.

Repair or Replace

A Fair Way to Weigh a Leaking Boiler

Repair is likely when

  • The leak is at a valve, pump or fitting.
  • The boiler body is dry.
  • The boiler is not near the end of its life.
  • The relief valve simply needs replacing.

Replacement is likely when

  • Water comes from the boiler body or a cracked section.
  • The unit is thirty or more years old and repairs recur.
  • Corrosion shows throughout.
  • A modern boiler would cut fuel use.

Boiler repair work describes the process and the checks a technician makes.

Frozen Pipes

When a Leak Follows a Heat Failure

A boiler that quits in a cold snap can let heating pipes freeze, and the thaw brings leaks. Check the hot water baseboards and radiators first, since the water inside them is the most exposed. If any pipe has split, close the water to the boiler, drain what you can and call.

Once the leak is stopped, emergency burst pipe repair replaces the split piece and tests the system. Wet floors and walls need quick drying, and water mitigation covers that.

Keep the house above freezing while you wait. A few space heaters in the rooms with pipes, used safely, can prevent a second failure. Never use a stove or oven to heat a home.

Before Next Winter

Fall Checks That Prevent Boiler Leaks

  1. Schedule a yearly service in the early fall, before the first cold week.
  2. Look at every visible joint, valve and fitting for drips, stains or white mineral crust.
  3. Test the relief valve by lifting its lever briefly, if the maker's manual allows it.
  4. Check the cold-fill pressure and compare it to the gauge's normal range.
  5. Bleed trapped air from radiators and replace worn valve packing.

Reading the System

How a Hot Water Boiler Heats a House and Where It Leaks

A hot water boiler heats water in a sealed loop. A circulator pump pushes the hot water through pipes to radiators or baseboards and back. An expansion tank absorbs the pressure rise as the water heats, and a relief valve opens if pressure gets too high. A fill valve tops up the loop to its set pressure when it drops.

Leaks follow the parts. Pumps leak at their flanges, valves at their stems, and old pipe at the joints where water, air and metal meet. A steam boiler works differently: it boils water into steam that rises to radiators and condenses back, so leaks show up at return lines and gauge glasses.

Knowing which type you have narrows the search. A technician looks first at the pressure gauge, the relief valve line and the expansion tank, then at the loop itself, because over-pressure from a failed tank is a frequent cause of leaks that look like pipe failure.

Code Note

What the Plumbing Code Says About Relief Valves

A boiler relief valve discharge must be piped to a safe spot and never capped. The International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the Maryland State Plumbing Code is built on, sets that rule. A technician who replaces a valve should match the pressure rating stamped on the boiler.
