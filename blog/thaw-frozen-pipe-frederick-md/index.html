How to Thaw a Frozen Pipe in a Frederick Home

By the Frederick Pro Plumbing team. Published 2026-10-03. Updated 2026-10-03.

Turn on the faucet, warm the pipe gently with a hair dryer or heating pad and keep the water off flames. Here is the full sequence and what comes next.

The Short Answer

To thaw a frozen pipe, open the faucet it serves, then apply gentle heat to the pipe starting nearest the faucet and working toward the frozen section. A hair dryer, a heating pad or warm towels work. Never use a torch, a propane heater or any open flame. Keep the faucet open until full flow returns, then check the pipe for splits.

If you cannot find the frozen section, the pipe is inside a wall or you see water spraying, close the main shutoff and call a plumber. Frederick County's building code puts the frost line at 30 inches, which keeps buried lines safe, but the pipes inside your house depend on your heat.

Local Note

Frozen Pipes Around Frederick

Frederick winters bring regular nights below freezing, and the pipes that fail first are the ones in garages, crawlspaces and exterior walls. Homes in Thurmont and Middletown often have longer runs to outbuildings and well houses, which are easy to forget until a cold snap.

If a pipe has already split, close the main shutoff first and then call. A fast emergency burst pipe repair limits the water damage.

Step by Step

Thawing a Frozen Pipe Safely

  1. Confirm it is frozen

    No water at one faucet while others work, a frosty pipe or a bulging section point to ice.

  2. Open the faucet

    Melting ice needs a place to flow. Open the tap, hot and cold.

  3. Apply gentle heat

    Use a hair dryer on low or a heating pad. Move slowly from the faucet end toward the blockage.

  4. Check for leaks

    When water flows, look along the pipe for drips or damp spots, then check other fixtures.

  5. Keep the heat on

    Raise the house temperature and open cabinet doors on exterior walls.

Local Note

Why Older Frederick Homes Freeze Sooner

Homes in the historic district and older streets often have supply pipes in exterior walls, built before insulation standards. Those pipes sit a few inches from brick or stone that stays cold for weeks. A two-night cold snap can freeze them even when the house feels warm.

Winter nights in the foothills near Thurmont can be colder than in town, and that is where the page on Thurmont plumbing focuses its advice about freeze calls. If you manage a rental or a second home, a thermostat that reports to your phone catches a heat failure before the pipes do.

Never Do This

Five Mistakes That Make a Frozen Pipe Worse

  1. Using a blowtorch, a propane heater or a heat gun at high setting. These start fires and can burst the pipe.
  2. Walking away with the faucet closed. Pressure builds behind the ice and splits the pipe.
  3. Thawing a pipe next to insulation or paper without clearing it away.
  4. Assuming one thawed pipe means all are clear. Pipes in the same wall often freeze together.
  5. Ignoring a bulge. A pipe that has swelled may already be cracked, and it will leak when it thaws.

Aftermath

What to Do Once the Pipe Is Thawed

A pipe that froze once will freeze again. Insulate it with foam sleeves or heat tape, seal air leaks around the wall and consider moving the pipe if it sits in an outside wall. A plumber can reroute a vulnerable supply line while a wall is open for another reason.

If the pipe did split, burst pipe repair work replaces the damaged section, and wet drywall or flooring needs water mitigation service within a day or two. A bill that jumps after a freeze can mean a hidden crack, and emergency leak detection can check the rest of the system.

Insurance is worth a call. Many homeowners policies cover sudden freeze damage if you kept the house heated. Photograph the split, keep the broken section and save receipts.

Before Winter

A Fall Checklist That Prevents Most Freeze Calls

Most freeze calls are preventable with an hour of work in October. Disconnect garden hoses, close the interior shutoff to each outdoor spigot and open the outdoor valve to drain it. Walk the crawlspace and basement with a flashlight and look for pipes touching exterior walls or sitting under vents. Wrap what you find in foam sleeves, and seal gaps where cold air enters.

Know where the main shutoff is before you need it. In many older Frederick homes it sits on the front foundation wall, beside the water heater or in a crawlspace. Test it once a year so it does not seize. A valve that will not turn in an emergency turns a thirty-second fix into a flood.

If you travel in winter, set the thermostat no lower than the mid-fifties, ask someone to check the house and consider a water leak sensor that sends an alert to your phone. A neighbor with a key and your phone number can turn the water off in minutes if a pipe lets go while you are away.

Choices

Quick Fixes and Lasting Fixes

Quick fixes for this cold snap

  • Let a faucet drip on the coldest line.
  • Open cabinet doors on exterior walls.
  • Add a space heater near the problem area, kept clear of anything flammable.
  • Raise the thermostat a few degrees.

Lasting fixes worth pricing

  • Foam or fiberglass insulation on exposed pipes.
  • Heat tape on vulnerable runs.
  • Rerouting a pipe out of an exterior wall.
  • Insulating the crawlspace and sealing rim joists.

A pipe that froze twice in one winter is a candidate for rerouting, not another thaw.
