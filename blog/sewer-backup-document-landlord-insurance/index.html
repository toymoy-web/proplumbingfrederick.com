Sewer Backup: What to Document for Your Landlord or Insurer

By the Frederick Pro Plumbing team. Published 2026-10-03. Updated 2026-10-03.

Photograph the water and the damage before cleanup, keep the plumber's invoice and camera video, and notify your landlord or insurer quickly in writing.

The Short Answer

After a sewer backup, document before you clean. Take wide and close photographs of the water level, the affected rooms and every damaged item, and record video of the drain or fixture where the water came up. Keep the plumber's invoice and, if a camera was used, ask for a copy of the video, because it shows the cause.

Notify your landlord or insurer in writing and keep a copy. The date and time you reported are part of the record. Do not throw away damaged belongings until the insurer or landlord has approved disposal.

Document

A Backup Documentation Checklist

  1. Photographs of each affected room from a doorway, then close-ups of damage.
  2. Video of the source drain, showing the water and any smell or color.
  3. A list of damaged items with brand, age and approximate value.
  4. The plumber's invoice, the date and time of arrival and the camera video if one was made.
  5. Copies of every message to the landlord or insurer, with dates.

Store copies in the cloud so you can find them if the phone is damaged.

Coverage

What Maryland's Insurance Regulator Says

The Maryland Insurance Administration says water that backs up through sewers or drains, when it is not due to the insured's negligence, is typically not covered under a standard homeowners policy. Coverage can be added as a water and sewer backup endorsement, which the agency says also protects a home if a sump pump overflows. Flood, groundwater and seepage are separate exclusions.

For tenants, the landlord's policy covers the building, not your belongings. Renters insurance covers belongings, but it may also exclude backup unless you added the endorsement. Check both before you assume anything is covered.

In older neighborhoods like the historic core of Walkersville and Frederick, laterals are decades old, so backup claims are common. Ask the landlord who is responsible for the sewer line, since that decides who pays for the plumbing repair.

Do and Do Not

Smart Moves and Mistakes After a Backup

Do

  • Photograph everything first.
  • Notify the landlord or insurer in writing.
  • Keep damaged items until approved.
  • Ask the plumber for a written cause.

Do not

  • Clean up before documenting.
  • Throw away belongings immediately.
  • Run water to see if it clears.
  • Skip the written notice.

A written report of the cause, ideally with camera video, is the strongest document in a claim.

The Repair

Getting the Line Fixed and the Records Together

The first call is for sewer backup repair work, which clears the line and traces the cause. If the camera shows roots or a crack, sewer line repair follows. Ask for the camera video and a written statement of what was found and fixed.

Sewage that reached carpet or drywall needs cleanup by a restoration crew, which is the work of water damage restoration service and has its own phone line. Keep its invoices and moisture logs too.

Time matters for the claim and for health. Sewage is contaminated water, and wet porous materials should be removed quickly. Give the insurer and the landlord the same set of photographs, and keep the originals.

Order

A Sensible Sequence After a Backup

  1. Make it safe

    Stop water use and keep people and pets out.

  2. Document

    Photograph and video before cleanup.

  3. Clear the line

    Call a plumber and get the cause in writing.

  4. Notify

    Write to the landlord or insurer with the date and cause.

  5. Clean and dry

    Hire cleanup once the insurer or landlord agrees.

Read Your Policy

Look for terms such as water backup, sewer and drain backup or sump overflow, and note the limit.

Writing the Notice

What to Say in a Written Notice to a Landlord or Insurer

A good notice is short and factual. State your name and address, the date and time you first noticed the backup, which fixtures were affected and what you did to stop it. Attach photographs and the plumber's invoice. Avoid guessing about the cause, since the camera or the plumber will establish it.

Ask for what you need in plain terms. Ask the landlord to confirm repair of the sewer line and arrange cleanup, and ask the insurer for a claim number and the next steps. Keep the tone calm. A clear record serves you better than an angry one.

Send the notice by email and by the method your lease or policy specifies, such as certified mail or a portal. Keep proof of sending, and follow up by phone with a note of whom you spoke to and when.

Health

Health Cautions After Sewage Reaches a Home

Contaminated water

Backup water carries bacteria and should be treated as hazardous.

Porous materials

Carpet, padding and drywall that soaked up sewage are usually discarded.

Children and pets

Keep them out of affected rooms until the area is cleaned and cleared.

Food and dishes

Throw out food and clean dishes that were exposed.

Hand washing

Wash thoroughly after any contact and before eating.

Illness

Tell a doctor if anyone feels sick in the days afterward.
