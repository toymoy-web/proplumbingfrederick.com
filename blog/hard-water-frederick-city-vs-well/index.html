Hard Water in Frederick: City Supply vs Private Well

By the Frederick Pro Plumbing team. Published 2026-10-03. Updated 2026-10-03.

City of Frederick water is slightly hard at roughly 100 mg/L, while private wells vary widely and can be far harder. A test tells you which you have.

The Short Answer

Published figures put the City of Frederick's water at about 99 to 101 mg/L of hardness, which is around six grains per gallon and counts as slightly hard on the USGS scale. That is enough to leave scale on fixtures over time but not enough to cause major trouble in most homes. Private wells are a different story, because hardness depends on the rock the water passes through.

Limestone and other carbonate rock, common in parts of the Frederick Valley, can produce much harder water. The only way to know what you have is to test. Hardness, iron, pH and bacteria are the usual tests for a well.

Where Your Water Comes From

City Water, Town Wells and Private Wells

The City of Frederick treats surface water from the Monocacy River, Linganore Creek and Fishing Creek Reservoir, and it also receives Potomac River water from the county's New Design plant. Each source has different minerals, which is why a published average hides some variation by neighborhood.

Other towns have their own systems. Thurmont uses groundwater wells, and so does Walkersville, where state assessments say the wells draw from carbonate rock and Glade Creek recharges about a quarter of the pumped water. Groundwater from limestone tends to be harder than river water, so ask each town for its annual water quality report.

Outside the towns, many Frederick County homes have private wells. The county health department's Well and Septic Branch keeps records on wells, and the homeowner is responsible for water quality. In New Market and the rural roads around it, wells are common and testing every year or two is a good habit.

Treatment

A Softener, a Filter or Nothing?

A softener tends to help when

  • Hardness is over about 120 mg/L.
  • You have a tankless heater.
  • Scale shows on fixtures and dishes.
  • You want longer appliance life.

You may not need one when

  • Hardness is near 100 mg/L and you see little scale.
  • Your main concern is chlorine taste, which a filter fixes.
  • You rent and cannot modify plumbing.
  • You flush the water heater yearly and are satisfied.

Water softener installation and filtration solve different problems.

What To Do

Steps to Take on City Water or a Well

On city water, start simple. Soak aerators and showerheads in vinegar, flush the water heater yearly and consider a softener only if scale really bothers you. A tankless heater is the main reason many owners decide to add one.

On a well, test first. Hardness, iron, pH, bacteria and nitrates each need a different answer. Iron needs a filter, acidic water needs a neutralizer and hardness needs a softener. If the pump or pressure tank also acts up, well pump repair service covers that side.

Maryland law says water conditioning equipment may be installed only by a licensed well driller, a water conditioner installer, a master plumber or a journeyman plumber, so ask who holds the license.

How It Wears

What Scale Does to Plumbing and Appliances Over Time

Scale forms when hard water heats up. Calcium carbonate comes out of solution and sticks to the nearest hot surface, which is the heating element, the burner end of a tank or the heat exchanger of a tankless unit. A quarter inch of scale can cut the efficiency of a heating element noticeably, because the metal cannot pass heat to the water.

Pipes suffer more slowly. Hot water lines gradually narrow, and fixtures with small openings, such as showerheads, aerators and valve cartridges, clog first. Dishwashers and washing machines collect scale inside, and you may see it as a white crust on the heating coil.

The practical effect is wear. A water heater in hard water can fail years sooner than the same model in soft water, and a tankless unit that is never descaled can shut down in two or three years. That is why even slightly hard water is worth managing with a yearly flush or a softener, and why the page on tankless water heater service work stresses annual descaling.

Test It

A Simple Way to Find Out Your Hardness

  1. Ask for the report

    City customers can read their utility's annual water quality report. Town systems publish their own.

  2. Use a test strip

    Hardware stores sell strips that give a rough reading in grains per gallon.

  3. Send a sample

    For wells, a state-certified lab gives exact numbers for hardness, iron, pH and bacteria.

  4. Compare to your symptoms

    Match the reading to scale, spotting and appliance wear.

Code Note

Standards for Water Treatment Products

Look for treatment equipment certified to NSF/ANSI standards, such as 44 for softeners, and for connections that follow the International Plumbing Code (IPC), including an air gap on the softener drain. Certification marks are on the unit or in its manual.
