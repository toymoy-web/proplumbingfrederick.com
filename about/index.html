Who We Are

What This Site Is and How It Works

Frederick Pro Plumbing is a local call line and information site for plumbing problems in Frederick, Maryland and the surrounding towns. The phone number on this site is answered around the clock. The dispatcher listens to what is happening, asks a few questions about the house and the problem, and connects the call to a plumbing professional who serves the area.

The site does not claim a license number, a founding year or customer reviews, because none are published here. The plumber who answers is responsible for holding the licenses Maryland and Frederick County require, and you are encouraged to ask for them. That is also the first piece of advice in the guides on this site.

As the advertising disclosure in the footer explains, this brand is paid for calls and leads. That does not change the advice here, but you should know it.

Before You Hire

Five Things to Check With Any Plumber in Maryland

  1. Ask for the Maryland master plumber license number. The State Board of Plumbing licenses plumbers, and the state keeps a public license lookup.
  2. In Frederick County, ask whether the master plumber also holds the county trade license and who pulls any permit.
  3. For heating work, ask for a Maryland HVACR license. For waterproofing or remodeling, ask for a Maryland Home Improvement Commission license.
  4. Get the price and the scope in writing before work starts.
  5. Ask about the warranty on parts and labor, and get it in writing.

A contractor who answers these plainly is a good sign.

Our Approach

How the Guides and Pages on This Site Are Written

Every page on this site is written for a specific Frederick-area situation. The service pages explain what the work involves, what drives the price and what to ask before you agree. The town pages describe the water, the housing and the weather of each place. The guides answer single questions in plain language.

Local facts come from public sources such as county and city agencies, the state insurance regulator and state licensing boards. Where a rule differs between the city, the county and the towns, the page says to confirm with the authority that covers your address, because that answer changes with the street.

The pages do not invent testimonials, ratings, awards or years in business. They also avoid promising arrival times, because no one can promise a clock time on a stormy night. What the line does promise is that a person answers, that you hear a price before work starts and that planned work gets a free estimate.
