Overview

How Shower and Bathtub Installation Works in Frederick

Shower and bathtub installation in Frederick is the plumbing behind a remodel. The old fixture comes out, and the supply valve, the drain and the trap are checked. A new valve is set at the right depth for the finished wall, the drain is connected and the new fixture is leveled and tested before anyone closes the wall or lays tile.

The details matter. Showers and tub-showers are required to have a pressure-balancing or thermostatic mixing valve that limits scalding when someone flushes a toilet or runs a washer. The rule follows the plumbing code that Maryland adopts, built on the International Plumbing Code, and the valve should be marked to the ASSE 1016 standard for these devices.

Drains are as important as valves. A tub or shower trap needs the correct size and slope, a vent and an access point where the layout allows. A trap that is too shallow lets sewer gas into the room, and one that is too deep slows the water.

Know the License

Plumbing Is One Part of a Bathroom Remodel

The Maryland Home Improvement Commission requires a license for most home improvement work, which includes remodeling a bathroom, tiling and waterproofing. The plumbing portion is separate and falls under the State Board of Plumbing. This line handles the plumbing: valves, drains, supply lines and fixture hookups.

Ask who holds the MHIC license for the remodel, who holds the master plumber license for the plumbing and who pulls the permit. Frederick County requires a plumbing permit for plumbing work, and shower drains buried in a slab get a groundwork inspection.

Before You Start

Questions to Settle Before the Tub Arrives

  1. Where is the drain, and does the new fixture match its position?
  2. Is the shutoff behind the wall accessible, or does the wall need an access panel?
  3. Which valve type is planned, and does it carry the right standard mark?
  4. Who handles the permit and the inspection, and who is on the job when the inspector comes?
  5. Is there a water test of the drain and a pressure test of the supply before walls are closed?

Answers to these save weeks and keep leaks out of the wall.
