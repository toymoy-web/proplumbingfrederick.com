Emergency Commercial Plumbing: Problems That Stop Business

A floor drain overflowing in a kitchen or back room

Overflow in a restaurant or shop is a health and safety issue and can close the business. Call at once.

A leak in a shared wall or a multi-unit building

Water in one unit becomes water in the next. Shut the building shutoff and call so the source is found.

Sewage or grease backing up from a grease trap or line

A full trap or a blocked line backs up into the floor. Stop use of fixtures and call.

A water heater or main failure in an occupied building

A failed commercial heater or a burst main in a building with tenants cannot wait for a business-hours appointment.

Overview

How Commercial Plumbing Works in Frederick

Commercial plumbing in Frederick differs from home service in a few ways. Fixtures are used heavily, lines are larger, and a failure closes the business. Permits matter more too. The City of Frederick states that all plumbing, gas and electrical work on commercial projects must be permitted and completed by a professional licensed with the city, and a tenant fit-out that touches plumbing needs the permits described on the city's pages.

The first call usually covers whether the problem is a blockage, a leak or a fixture failure. A restaurant with a slow kitchen drain gets a camera and often hydro jetting. A retail shop with no hot water gets a heater check. An apartment building with several units backing up gets a main line inspection, because the blockage affects many tenants.

Records are part of the service. Test reports, permit numbers and invoices should be filed, because landlords, insurers and inspectors ask for them. Keep a log of grease line cleaning and backflow tests for the building file.

Grease and Compliance

Why Kitchens Need Scheduled Cleaning, Not Emergency Calls

The county utility's grease guidance puts the burden on the owner when a blockage forms on private property. For a restaurant, that means the grease trap and the lines leading to it need service on a calendar, not after a backup.

Downtown Frederick has many restaurants in older buildings with old drains, and a lunch rush that finds the floor drain overflowing is costly. A schedule of jetting every six to twelve months, set by how much cooking the kitchen does, costs far less than a weekend emergency. Hydro jetting service is the usual tool for the main line, and a camera run tells you whether the pipe can take it.

Maintenance

A Short Preventive Checklist: What to Do Before an Emergency

  1. Schedule grease line and trap cleaning on a calendar based on kitchen volume.
  2. Test backflow devices on the schedule the utility sets and file the reports.
  3. Know where the main shutoff and each tenant shutoff are, and label them.
  4. Inspect water heaters and flush them yearly, and replace those past their expected life.
  5. Train staff not to flush wipes, paper towels or kitchen waste, and post a sign in restrooms.

A one-page plan on the wall of the utility room helps whoever is on duty at 2 a.m. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) that Maryland follows sets the minimums for fixtures, grease interceptors and backflow protection, and the permit office applies it.
